# SHAKTI Principal Directive PD-01 — Mandatory Agentic AI Swarm Systems

**Status:** Founder-designated mandatory, non-waivable principal directive.  
**Applicability:** Every project, platform, subsystem, phase, engineering activity and operational workflow governed by SHAKTI Standards.

## Non-negotiable requirement
Every SHAKTI-governed system MUST be designed, engineered, tested and operated with a coordinated Agentic AI Swarm architecture. No project, phase, module, team or delivery owner may omit, waive, downgrade or silently defer this requirement. Project-specific implementation may vary; compliance may not. Incomplete implementation is a SHAKTI non-conformance and blocks the applicable readiness/release gate. Staged implementation is permitted; exemption is not.

## Minimum agent responsibilities
- **Orchestrator:** decomposes objectives, delegates work, manages dependencies and coordinates completion.
- **Domain specialists:** execute bounded tasks for the project's domain and workflows.
- **Research/evidence:** gathers traceable evidence, evaluates source quality and records uncertainty.
- **Engineering/operations:** supports implementation, testing, maintenance and execution.
- **Security/safety/compliance:** checks permissions, threats, statutory requirements and policy boundaries.
- **Quality/verification:** checks outputs against acceptance criteria and evidence, independently where needed.
- **Monitoring/recovery:** detects failures, manages bounded retries/recovery and escalates unresolved risks.
- **Reporting/audit:** preserves decisions, provenance, task history, results and limitations.

These are functional responsibilities, not necessarily separate models or services. Roles may share a runtime only where separation does not undermine verification, separation of duties or safety.

## Mandatory controls
Each swarm MUST implement and evidence, proportionate to risk:
1. Defined agent identities, responsibilities, permissions and least-privilege access.
2. Authoritative task state and shared evidence/provenance record.
3. Dependency-aware orchestration, prioritisation, idempotency and duplicate-work controls.
4. Input validation, source trust boundaries and prompt/instruction-injection protection.
5. Independent verification for material, safety-critical, financial, legal or regulated outputs.
6. Bounded retries, timeouts, queue limits, failure isolation and human escalation.
7. Durable audit logs, versioning, traceability and reproducible outcomes.
8. Human approval gates for consequential actions and controlled release.
9. Cost, latency, resource, model and service-availability monitoring.
10. Safe degraded/fallback mode when models, tools, networks or agents are unavailable.
11. Data protection, tenant isolation, secrets handling and retention controls.
12. Continuous evaluation for quality, drift, bias, hallucination, security and operational effectiveness.

## Autonomy and reserved authority
Agents may autonomously perform authorised research, analysis, drafting, internal coordination, code changes and tests within approved environments. Autonomy does not confer approval authority. Production deployment, destructive data changes, financial commitments, purchases, external legal/regulatory submissions, binding representations, physical actuation, safety-critical control and product/quality release require applicable explicit human, statutory, professional and change-control approvals. Agents must never impersonate approvers or manufacture approval evidence.

## Lifecycle application
Applies throughout discovery, context, evidence, compliance, feasibility, validation, design, engineering, testing, approval, certification, deployment, operation, monitoring, measurement and continuous assurance. Reassess after material changes in scope, risk, data, models, integrations or operating conditions.

## Required evidence and acceptance
Maintain a versioned swarm register containing agent/role inventory and permissions; orchestration and task/data-flow architecture; human approval/escalation matrix; security/safety/privacy/compliance threat assessment; normal/adversarial/failure/recovery test results; independent verification and unresolved defects; cost/resource and availability assessment; monitoring, incident, fallback and change-control runbooks; and TRL/readiness assessment for technology-bearing components and the integrated system where applicable.

A proposal, diagram or demo is not an implemented or operational swarm. State status truthfully and support claims with evidence. No project may claim SHAKTI conformance or pass a relevant readiness/release gate while this mandatory architecture or its applicable controls are absent or unverified.

**Principal rule:** ONE SHAKTI STANDARD. MANY PROJECT-SPECIFIC OPERATING SYSTEMS. MANDATORY COORDINATED AGENTIC AI SWARMS THROUGHOUT.
