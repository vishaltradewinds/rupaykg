## 1. Product objective

RupayKg is to provide one shared, evidence-led reporting infrastructure for environmental operations across Urban and Rural India. It must reuse the existing domain, evidence, MRV, statutory-applicability, EPR, ESG, geography, identity, and tenant-security capabilities rather than create a second reporting data platform.

The stakeholder chain is mandatory:

**Generator → Aggregator → Transporter → Recycler / Processor**, connected with **BWGs, ULBs, Gram Panchayats, District and State authorities, producers/importers/brand owners, enterprises, auditors/verifiers, buyers, CSR and government/PSU users**.

Reports must distinguish:
- measured facts from estimates;
- source evidence from derived calculations;
- user-reported external references from independently verified references;
- internal readiness from official submission, authority decision, certificate issuance or acceptance;
- a report generated by RupayKg from a report accepted by an external authority.

## 3. Reporting product catalogue

### A. Regulatory and statutory reporting
1. Solid Waste Management Rules, 2026 — generator, BWG, facility, local body, rural and urban reporting support.
2. Plastic Waste EPR.
3. E-Waste EPR.
4. Battery Waste EPR.
5. Waste Tyre EPR.
6. Used Oil EPR.
7. End-of-Life Vehicle reporting.
8. Construction and Demolition Waste reporting.
9. Biomedical, hazardous and other separately regulated waste streams — separate applicability and evidence adapters, not folded into ordinary municipal solid waste.
10. State/local by-law and authorization reporting, only where a current authoritative rule source and jurisdiction are configured.

### B. Digital MRV, audit and verification
1. Activity, measurement and evidence register.
2. Generator-to-aggregator-to-transporter-to-processor chain of custody.
3. Weighbridge and measurement reconciliation.
4. Facility input/output/reject/residual mass balance.
5. Evidence completeness, provenance and quality report.
6. Verification decisions, exceptions, corrective actions and audit trail.
7. Methodology version, calculation lineage and report reproducibility.
8. Guardian/Hedera provenance references where independently confirmed; blockchain anchoring is not itself proof that the underlying activity is true.

### C. Carbon and climate reporting
1. Organizational/facility/project GHG inventory, with explicit boundary and methodology.
2. Scope 1, Scope 2 and relevant Scope 3 data preparation.
3. Methane and landfill-related estimates, with assumptions and uncertainty.
4. Avoided-emissions and diversion estimates, with baseline and methodology.
5. CCTS / Indian Carbon Market reporting support for applicable entities and approved methodologies.
6. Carbon project monitoring and verification evidence packs.
7. Carbon-credit status reporting only from the relevant authorized registry/issuer evidence.

### D. Corporate ESG and sustainability
1. SEBI BRSR and BRSR Core, where applicable.
2. GRI disclosures and crosswalks.
3. IFRS S1 / S2 disclosure support.
4. CDP climate/water disclosures.
5. TCFD-aligned climate-risk disclosures.
6. TNFD nature-related disclosures.
7. UN SDG contribution indicators.
8. Integrated sustainability and impact reporting.

Each framework needs a versioned disclosure catalogue, applicability assessment, reporting boundary, period, data lineage, evidence requirements, calculation/mapping rules, review status and export schema. A crosswalk is not equivalence or certification.

### E. Government, municipal and rural reporting
1. ULB, municipal corporation and municipal council ward-wise waste reports.
2. Gram Panchayat and District Panchayat village-level reports.
3. District and State consolidated environmental performance.
4. Urban/Rural mode-specific service and infrastructure reports.
5. Facility, project and mission monitoring.
6. Government/PSU institutional waste and sustainability reports.
7. Public-programme outcome and impact reports, only against defined indicators and source evidence.

### F. Circular economy and material-flow reporting
1. Waste generation, segregation, collection, transfer, receipt and processing.
2. Material flow analysis and mass balance.
3. Reuse, recycling, recovery and reject rates.
4. Landfill diversion and residual disposal.
5. Organic waste, composting and biomethanation.
6. Recycled material output and destination.
7. Reverse logistics and industrial symbiosis.
8. Material-wise and geography-wise circularity indicators.

### G. Financial, commercial and impact reporting
1. Collection, transport and processing cost per tonne.
2. Facility throughput, utilization and unit economics.
3. Material recovery value and revenue.
4. Project CAPEX/OPEX, ROI and payback where verified financial inputs exist.
5. Municipal service cost and expenditure.
6. EPR obligation/evidence reconciliation (not certificate issuance).
7. CSR funding, outputs and evidenced outcomes.
8. Environmental and social impact indicators.

Financial outputs must identify source, period, currency, tax treatment where relevant, and whether values are actual, estimated or forecast.

## 4. Stakeholder report views

| Stakeholder | Minimum report set |
|---|---|
| Generator | Waste generated/segregated/handed over; compliance readiness; missing evidence |
| Aggregator | Collection batches; source and destination; custody transfer; reconciliation |
| Transporter | Dispatch/receipt; vehicle and route evidence where captured; delivery exceptions |
| Recycler / Processor | Inbound, processed, recovered, rejected and residual quantities; facility returns |
| BWG | Eligibility assessment, waste ledger, handover, processing and return readiness |
| ULB / Municipal Council | Ward-wise generation, collection, transport, processing, disposal and service KPIs |
| Gram Panchayat | Village-level waste collection, segregation, processing and residuals |
| District / State | Consolidated performance with drill-down, source coverage and unresolved data |
| Producer / Importer / Brand Owner | EPR applicability, obligation period, eligible evidence and external certificate references |
| Enterprise / Industry | Waste, EPR, BRSR, GHG and environmental compliance evidence |
| Auditor / Verifier | Evidence manifest, calculation lineage, exceptions, approvals and immutable history |
| CSR / Investor / Buyer | Funding, outputs, outcomes, material provenance and claim limitations |
| Government / PSU | Institution and programme performance, evidence coverage and compliance readiness |

Every view must be filtered by verified identity, organization membership, role permission, active organization and applicable geography. A dashboard filter is not a substitute for server-side authorization and database tenant isolation.

## 5. Common report engine contract

Every report definition must include:

- `report_code`, title, description and owner;
- framework/regime and version;
- jurisdiction and applicability rule/version;
- stakeholder and permitted role;
- organization/site/facility/geography scope;
- reporting period, timezone and cutoff;
- required source entities and evidence types;
- calculation/mapping rules and units;
- data-quality and reconciliation rules;
- evidence manifest and source lineage;
- preparer, reviewer, verifier and approval requirements;
- output schema and supported export formats;
- external submission boundary and status provenance.

Every generated report instance must preserve:
- immutable report/run identifier and generation timestamp;
- report definition and methodology versions;
- exact source record IDs and evidence hashes;
- calculation inputs, outputs, units and assumptions;
- missing, estimated, disputed and unresolved fields;
- review decisions and actor identity;
- file hash and export metadata;
- supersession/correction relationship to prior runs.

### Required report states

`DRAFT → VALIDATED → READY_FOR_REVIEW → REVIEWED → APPROVED_INTERNAL → EXPORTED`

External lifecycle is separate:

`NOT_SUBMITTED → USER_REPORTED_SUBMITTED → PORTAL_ACKNOWLEDGED → AUTHORITY_REVIEW → ACCEPTED / REJECTED / CORRECTION_REQUIRED`

Only authoritative external evidence may move an external state beyond user-reported. Keep `UNRESOLVED`, `INCOMPLETE`, `REJECTED` and `SUPERSEDED` explicit. Do not collapse these into a single `verified` boolean.

## 6. Implementation sequence

### Phase 0 — reuse and gap audit
- Map each report family to existing tables, routes, services, panels and tests.
- Identify existing exports, report queries and permission checks.
- Identify duplicate calculations and competing sources of truth.
- Confirm current Render deployment and exact deployed commit before UI or release claims.
- Do not modify Neon production or run migrations during this phase.

### Phase 1 — reporting foundation
- Create a versioned report catalogue and report-definition contract.
- Define common report-run metadata, provenance, data-quality and status semantics.
- Reuse existing tenant-scoped data access and evidence models.
- Add report permission matrix and server-side authorization tests.
- Define reusable PDF/CSV/XLSX export contracts only after the existing export stack is inspected.

### Phase 2 — first operational release
Prioritize:
1. CPCB SWM 2026 operational and return-readiness reports (Urban and Rural).
2. Generator → Aggregator → Transporter → Recycler chain-of-custody and mass-balance reports.
3. Digital MRV evidence and verification pack.
4. EPR obligation/evidence reconciliation.
5. BRSR/BRSR Core structured disclosures.
6. Facility, ULB, Gram Panchayat and district summary reports.

### Phase 3 — framework expansion
- GRI, IFRS S1/S2, CDP, TCFD and TNFD crosswalks.
- CCTS/carbon methodology adapters.
- Other EPR and separately regulated waste-stream adapters.
- CSR, financial, SDG and development-impact reports.

### Phase 4 — authorized external integrations
- Obtain official interface documentation, written permission, credentials and sandbox.
- Implement the approved API contract and audit controls.
- Keep connectors disabled until UAT, security review and release approval.
- Never scrape portals, bypass CAPTCHA/OTP, replay user sessions or infer an API from a public user guide.

## 7. Acceptance gates

### Functional
- Correct period, geography, stakeholder and material filters.
- Reproducible totals from identified source records.
- Explicit units and deterministic conversion rules.
- Mass-balance reconciliation and duplicate-event handling.
- Correct report states and exception visibility.
- Export file opens and matches on-screen totals.
- Corrections create a new report version; previous runs remain traceable.

### Security and privacy
- Verified identity and organization membership required.
- Tenant and geography isolation tested at API and database layers.
- No cross-tenant leakage in dashboards, generated files, download links or cached exports.
- Export access is authorized and audited.
- Sensitive evidence access follows its classification and retention rules.

### Evidence and integrity
- No estimated value presented as measured.
- No missing value silently converted to zero.
- No user-entered external reference promoted to verified.
- No official certificate, portal acceptance, assurance or carbon-credit issuance claimed without issuer evidence.
- Environmental attributes cannot be double counted across reports or claims.

### Operational
- Unit, integration, authorization, tenant-isolation, export and browser tests pass.
- CI is green on the exact proposed PR head.
- Render preview/deployment is checked against the exact commit and existing production configuration.
- Neon production remains unchanged until separately authorized migration/release gates are met.

## 8. Regulatory and claims boundary

RupayKg is a reporting, evidence, operational and integration platform. It can prepare reports and evidence packs. It must not represent an internal report as a statutory filing, government approval, official certificate, independent assurance opinion, verified carbon credit or regulator acceptance.

The CPCB SWM 2026 mapping already in this repository records that the central portal/competent authority retains statutory actions and that the public guide reviewed does not establish a public API. Confirm current official rules, amendments, local by-laws, applicability and reporting formats before enabling a production compliance conclusion.

ICVCM Core Carbon Principles are an integrity benchmark, not a substitute for CPCB, EPR, CCTS or a carbon registry's own rules. GRI, IFRS, CDP, TCFD and TNFD mappings must preserve each framework's scope and requirements; cross-mapping does not imply equivalence.
