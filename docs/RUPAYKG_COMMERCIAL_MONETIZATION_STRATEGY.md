# RupayKg Commercial Monetization — Product, Services and Dual-DPI Ecosystem Strategy

**Effective:** 2026-11-07  
**Authority:** Commercial strategy proposal  
**Core rule:** Monetization first; use open standards, DPGA-listed technology and DPI implementation learning where they improve commercial value and customer outcomes.

## 1. Positioning

RupayKg is a proprietary commercial operating platform for environmental resource flows, compliance operations and digital MRV. Its objective is to become an interoperable environmental operating platform that can be deployed within government-led digital ecosystems under their applicable institutional, legal, procurement, security and data-governance controls.

DIGICORE INFRASYS is the intended engineering, product and commercialisation company for RupayKg and related products. Its role is to own or lawfully hold the relevant IP rights, build and maintain the products, deliver implementation and integrations, and provide managed services. Actual ownership must be evidenced through entity records, IP assignments, contributor agreements, trademarks where applicable and third-party licence compliance; this strategy is not itself an IP assignment or legal opinion.

RupayKg sells software, operational efficiency, traceability, integration, reporting, managed services and support. It does not claim to be a public regulator, statutory approval body, accredited verifier, official carbon-credit issuer or government DPI operator.

## 2. Two DPI ecosystems — different jobs, one strategy

| Ecosystem / platform | Use it for | Output to the RupayKg business |
|---|---|---|
| DPGA Registry | Discover reusable digital software, recognised DPGs, technology components, standards and candidate source repositories | Shortlisted, licence-reviewed components and potential connector/service opportunities |
| Global DPI Repository (GDPIR) | Study proven DPI implementation patterns, institutional governance, maturity, deployment, funding, sustainability and lessons | Reference architecture decisions, public-sector deployment patterns, governance controls and implementation service opportunities |
| RupayKg | Convert technically and institutionally validated findings into an environmental operating product | Commercial software modules, APIs, customer deployments, reporting and managed environmental operations |
| DIGICORE INFRASYS | Engineer, lawfully own/control, implement and commercialise the platform and service capability | Product/IP value, contracts, recurring revenue, implementation revenue, integration and managed-services business |

**Non-substitution rule:** DPGA is primarily a digital technology discovery/reuse source. GDPIR is primarily a DPI implementation and governance learning source. Neither is a substitute for the other; neither automatically validates a RupayKg product or creates customer demand.

### 2.1 DPGA Registry — software and technology sourcing

For every candidate, verify the exact solution boundary, recognition status, upstream repository, release/version, licence for each relevant artefact, dependency tree, platform requirements, APIs/data formats, maintenance, security, hosting costs, support, commercial-use obligations and exit path.

A DPGA listing is a discovery signal—not automatic production approval, procurement availability, security assurance for our deployment, statutory suitability or a commercial endorsement. Do not assume a profile-level licence accurately covers every code/data/documentation component. Inspect the exact upstream release and notices.

### 2.2 Global DPI Repository — implementation and governance research

For every relevant reference implementation, capture the country and sector, public sponsor and operator, legal mandate, governance bodies, participant roles, institutional agreements, architecture and building blocks, interfaces and standards, data governance, privacy/security safeguards, maturity and adoption evidence, rollout stages, financing/procurement, operating model, maintenance, sustainability and implementation lessons.

Record whether source code, APIs, specifications, datasets or reuse rights are actually available. Do not assume GDPIR is a software marketplace, procurement portal, runtime API dependency or an open-source catalogue. A public implementation model must be legally and institutionally adapted before use in India or any customer deployment.

### 2.3 Conversion into commercial value

Each finding must become one of the following controlled decisions: reject, watch, prototype, validate with a named customer, approve for implementation, live, or retire. Before adoption, require a defined customer use case, a measurable outcome, legal/licence review, architecture fit, security assessment, operating owner, total cost, buyer/budget path and commercial model.

The detailed operating method is in `docs/DPI_ECOSYSTEM_STRATEGY_DIGICORE_INFRASYS_RUPAYKG.md`.

## 3. Product packages

| Package | Customer | Revenue model |
|---|---|---|
| Resource Flow Operations | ULBs, waste generators, collectors, processors and recyclers | Annual organisation/site subscription; onboarding and support |
| Compliance Operations | Bulk waste generators, producers/brand owners, enterprises and public bodies, subject to applicability | Annual module subscription; reporting-cycle and facility bands |
| Digital MRV Operations | Waste, landfill, biomass and circular-economy project developers | Project/site onboarding plus recurring monitoring and evidence-management subscription |
| Enterprise / Government | Multi-site organisations, states, ULBs and PSUs | Annual contract, implementation, SLA and managed operations |
| API / Integration | Software providers, system integrators, large customers and public agencies | Setup, integration maintenance, usage bands and support |
| Managed Data & Reporting | Customers needing data curation, reconciliation, audit packages or public dataset publication | Monthly/annual service scope |
| Partner / OEM | Device, weighing, laboratory, audit and technology partners | Integration, reseller/OEM or support contracts |

## 4. Revenue principles

- Price by measurable service value, facility/site count, organisation, reporting period, API tier, project workload and SLA.
- Keep one-time implementation revenue separate from recurring subscription and managed-service revenue.
- Do not invent prices before customer, budget, competitor and cost-to-serve validation.
- Do not depend on carbon-credit success fees as the primary business model.
- Never charge for favourable verification, statutory approval, certificate issuance or regulator acceptance.
- Preserve data export, customer exit, confidentiality and lawful access. Customer data is not an asset for resale.
- Clearly price hosting, data volume, hardware, travel, third-party providers, support and taxes.

## 5. Defensible commercial assets and DIGICORE INFRASYS responsibilities

Keep proprietary by default, subject to actual rights and third-party obligations:
- production platform source and deployment automation;
- tenant, identity, organisation, geography and access control plane;
- statutory applicability and compliance workflow implementation;
- methodology adapters, calculation orchestration and audit trace implementation;
- evidence vault, quality gates and verification workflow;
- registry, credential, transfer, retirement and settlement orchestration;
- analytics, commercial reporting and customer-specific configuration;
- device, trust-provider and external-system adapters;
- deployment, monitoring, backup, support and managed-operation playbooks;
- pricing, packaging, billing and partner-management services.

DIGICORE INFRASYS should maintain the IP register, source provenance, contribution/assignment records, software bill of materials, third-party notices, security and release records, product support obligations and customer contracts. This is an IP boundary policy, not a legal conclusion that all listed assets are already owned by the company. Third-party data, public standards, government publications, open-source dependencies and customer-owned information remain governed by their own rights and contracts.

## 6. What to publish openly

Open selectively, after legal and security review:
- API specifications and integration contracts;
- neutral data schemas and data dictionaries;
- interoperability mappings;
- non-personal synthetic examples and conformance fixtures;
- supported connector documentation;
- selected implementation guides that do not disclose trade secrets or security-sensitive internals.

An open specification is not an open-source implementation. A published API does not require publishing the private implementation that serves it.

## 7. DPG/DPGA and DPI ecosystem policy

1. Do not make DPG recognition the primary commercial objective.
2. Do not add an open-source licence or remove package-private settings in the commercial repository without explicit rights-holder approval.
3. Do not extract and publish the existing domain/carbon packages wholesale.
4. Use selected recognised DPGs as separately governed components or services where they solve a customer need.
5. Keep their licences, notices, security, hosting, support and maintenance obligations explicit.
6. Treat DPGA recognition as optional; nominate only a separately scoped product if the strategic benefit outweighs loss of exclusivity and all DPGA criteria are evidenced.
7. Do not imply DPGA partnership or government endorsement through use of a listed DPG.
8. Treat GDPIR as a source of implementation and governance learning, not a runtime dependency or procurement endorsement.
9. Do not claim RupayKg is itself a DPI, government operator, statutory registry or public authority without a separate formal institutional and legal basis.
10. Government-led ecosystem integration requires authorised interfaces, valid data-sharing rights, security review, procurement/contract authority and customer acceptance.

## 8. Monetizable integration and implementation services

- OpenFn connector implementation and managed workflow operations, subject to exact version/licence validation.
- ODK partner field-data onboarding and controlled ingestion.
- Sunbird RC or CREDEBL configuration and credential interoperability, if a real use case justifies one.
- CKAN-based managed open environmental data portals, with separate deployment and AGPL compliance.
- X-Road integration advisory and deployment support only for institutionally governed government federation programmes.
- DPI architecture assessment and ecosystem integration planning.
- Government/ULB/PSU and enterprise onboarding, deployment and operational enablement.
- API, connector, data migration and interoperability engineering.
- Data quality, transformation, reconciliation and source-to-report assurance.
- Managed hosting/operations, support, monitoring, backup/recovery, training and SLA services.

DIGICORE INFRASYS charges for its own engineering, deployment, configuration, hosting, integration, support and service commitments—not for the public DPGA listing, public infrastructure access, favourable verification or a free right to use a DPG.

## 9. Commercial execution metrics

Track:
- contracted ARR and recurring revenue mix;
- qualified pipeline, conversion, average contract value and sales cycle;
- gross margin by SaaS, implementation and managed service;
- onboarding cost and time-to-value;
- active paying organisations and facilities;
- renewals, churn and net revenue retention;
- cost to serve per site, data volume and reporting cycle;
- integration revenue and partner-sourced pipeline;
- support cost, uptime, incident response and cash collection;
- connector reuse, deployment repeatability and time/cost per new jurisdiction.

## 10. Immediate management and engineering decisions

- Keep `vishaltradewinds/rupaykg` `main` authoritative.
- Keep national deployment objectives as the primary architecture focus; Jabalpur remains the first field implementation of that same architecture.
- Use DPGA for software/technology discovery and GDPIR for governance, architecture, maturity and deployment-model research.
- Record source, date, artefact and evidence for each candidate; do not assume GDPIR API/download/licence availability.
- Use a cached, attributed DPGA Registry API snapshot only for a future curated integration catalogue; no runtime dependency.
- Prioritise an OpenFn technical/licence assessment and an ODK import contract as discovery tasks, not production approvals.
- Do not replace existing RupayKg registries, field app, evidence store, identity or MRV workflows without a validated customer requirement and controlled migration plan.
- Require a named buyer/use case, budget owner, measurable value and cost-to-serve before production integration.
- Preserve applicable privacy, security, tenant isolation, regulatory and independent acceptance gates.
- Keep production, statutory, certification, field and TRL gates unchanged.

## 11. Commercial decision

**RupayKg remains a proprietary commercial environmental operating platform, engineered and commercialised by DIGICORE INFRASYS subject to documented rights and contracts.** DPGA helps discover reusable technology; GDPIR helps learn infrastructure implementation and governance; RupayKg turns validated learning into a customer-ready product and paid services. Open standards and carefully selected DPGs improve interoperability and reduce duplication. Proprietary workflows, deployment capability, operational evidence, service quality, support and customer relationships sustain the business.

**Status:** Internal strategy proposal; not a price list, signed customer contract, revenue forecast, proof of legal IP ownership, DPGA recognition, DPI designation, government endorsement or legal opinion.
