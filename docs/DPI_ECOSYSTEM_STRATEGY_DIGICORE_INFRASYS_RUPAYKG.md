# Joint DPI Ecosystem Strategy — DIGICORE INFRASYS and RupayKg

**Effective:** 2026-11-07  
**Authority:** Strategic direction for ecosystem research, product architecture and commercialisation  
**Status:** Internal strategy baseline. This document is not a government partnership, DPI designation, procurement award, regulatory approval or proof of implementation readiness.

## 1. Strategic direction

Use the two DPI ecosystems together, for different and complementary purposes:

- **DPGA Registry:** discover reusable digital software, platforms, standards and technology components. Evaluate them for technical fit, licence compatibility, ownership, security, maintainability, portability and total cost.
- **Global DPI Repository (GDPIR):** study real-world digital public infrastructure implementations and their institutional context: architecture, governance, operating models, maturity, deployment, funding, sustainability and lessons.
- **RupayKg:** translate the resulting evidence into a commercially viable, interoperable environmental operating platform and paid integration/implementation services.
- **DIGICORE INFRASYS:** act as the engineering, product, implementation and managed-services company intended to own or hold the appropriate rights to, build, operate and commercialise RupayKg and related products.

**Strategic objective:** Develop RupayKg as a commercially sustainable, interoperable environmental operating platform that can be deployed within government-led digital ecosystems.

The commercial platform should be capable of supporting public programmes and independent ecosystem participants through governed interfaces. This does not mean RupayKg is itself a government DPI, regulator, statutory authority, public registry or official approval body.

## 2. Distinct roles — do not conflate the ecosystems

| Layer | Primary question | What it contributes | What it does not prove |
|---|---|---|---|
| DPGA Registry | Which reusable software/technology already exists? | Candidate DPGs, source repositories, licences, documentation, technical building blocks and discovery leads | Operational success in India, statutory suitability, procurement availability, support commitments or customer demand |
| Global DPI Repository | How have public digital infrastructure systems been designed and deployed? | Reference implementations, governance and institutional arrangements, maturity and deployment models, public-sector lessons | That the referenced system is a software component ready to install, or that its model can be copied without local legal and institutional approval |
| RupayKg | What environmental operating capability can be productised and sold? | Resource-flow operations, compliance workflows, digital MRV, evidence, integrations, data services and auditable reporting | Regulator authority, independent verification, statutory approval or official issuance unless separately authorised |
| DIGICORE INFRASYS | Who turns the product and learning into a sustainable business? | Product engineering, architecture, IP stewardship, implementation, systems integration, hosting/managed operations, support and commercial contracts | Ownership of third-party software/data, or government endorsement, unless legally evidenced |

The DPGA Registry is primarily a **technology discovery and reuse source**. GDPIR is primarily an **infrastructure implementation and governance learning source**. One must not be used as a substitute for the other.

## 3. Dual-source research and decision workflow

### Track A — DPGA technology discovery

For each candidate component, capture:

1. Registry identity, current recognition status, exact solution boundary and official source links.
2. Source repository, release/version, commit or artefact provenance and maintenance activity.
3. Exact licence for the specific code, data, documentation, model and dependency versions proposed for use.
4. Dependency, hosting, platform and proprietary-service requirements.
5. API and data contracts, standards, import/export, portability and interoperability.
6. Security posture, vulnerability disclosures, update cadence, support and operational ownership.
7. Deployment effort, infrastructure requirements, operating cost and total cost of ownership.
8. Commercial-use compatibility, attribution, modification, distribution, hosted-service and contribution obligations.
9. RupayKg use case, customer value, integration effort, replacement risk and exit plan.
10. Decision: reject, watch, prototype, adopt for a named customer, or operate as a separately governed service.

DPGA recognition is a discovery signal, not automatic approval for production reuse. A registry listing must never replace inspection of the exact upstream release and its licence.

### Track B — GDPIR implementation and governance learning

For each relevant DPI reference implementation, capture the evidence available for:

1. Country, sector, public purpose, use case and target population.
2. Public sponsor, accountable operator, legal/institutional mandate and governance bodies.
3. Roles of government agencies, private providers, civil society, participants and independent oversight.
4. Institutional agreements, participation rules, onboarding, dispute handling and accountability.
5. Architecture, building blocks, interfaces, data exchange and interoperability standards.
6. Identity, payment, registry, credential, trust and consent patterns where relevant.
7. Privacy, data protection, security, sovereignty and lawful data-access controls.
8. Maturity, deployment stages, actual coverage/adoption evidence and operating performance.
9. Funding, procurement, unit economics, ongoing operations, maintenance and sustainability.
10. Source code, specifications, APIs, documentation and reuse rights where explicitly available.
11. Implementation challenges, failure modes, local adaptation and lessons for scale.
12. Potential relevance to RupayKg and a specific commercial implementation/service opportunity.

Do not assume that GDPIR entries expose source code, APIs, downloadable datasets or open licences. Record what is actually available for each entry and identify evidence gaps. Treat GDPIR as a learning and comparative reference, not a runtime dependency or vendor/procurement marketplace.

### Track C — Translation into RupayKg

Every finding from Tracks A or B must be translated into a documented product decision:

- Reusable software component: assess integration into a replaceable adapter or separately operated service.
- Architecture pattern: convert into a technology-neutral design requirement and testable architecture decision.
- Governance model: map to the proposed operator, customer, government and participant roles; obtain legal/institutional review.
- Data exchange pattern: publish or version an appropriate schema/API contract; enforce identity, provenance, purpose, access and tenant controls.
- Deployment pattern: adapt into repeatable national, state, district, ULB, organisation, facility and site deployment configurations.
- Maturity/operations lesson: convert into measurable service-level, support, resilience, security, monitoring and acceptance criteria.
- Commercial opportunity: identify a named buyer/user, budget owner, procurement/contract path, value proposition, cost-to-serve and pricing model.

No item moves from research to production merely because it appears in either ecosystem.

## 4. DIGICORE INFRASYS — company and commercialisation role

DIGICORE INFRASYS is the intended commercial engineering and operating company for this strategy. Its role is to:

- own or hold documented rights to proprietary product code, brands, designs, deployment automation and company-developed IP;
- maintain the IP register, contributor/employee/contractor assignments, third-party licences, notices and software bill of materials;
- fund and direct product engineering, architecture, security, quality assurance and release management;
- convert standards and public technology into customer-ready products and integration offerings;
- contract for implementation, migration, integration, configuration, hosting and managed operations;
- operate customer support, service management, monitoring, incident response, backup and recovery;
- develop partner, OEM, systems-integrator and public-sector delivery channels;
- maintain commercial accountability through recurring revenue, delivery margin, renewals and cost-to-serve.

**Rights and entity evidence gate:** the strategic intention that DIGICORE INFRASYS owns/commercialises the products must be supported by the actual legal entity, trademark registrations (where applicable), IP assignments, employment/contractor agreements, third-party licences and customer contracts. This strategy document is not itself an IP assignment or legal opinion. Third-party code, standards, public data and customer data remain subject to their own rights and terms.

## 5. RupayKg product and service model

RupayKg is the environmental product platform through which DIGICORE INFRASYS can deliver:

### Product capabilities
- Resource-flow operations across generators, collectors, transporters, processors, recyclers and facilities.
- Environmental and waste compliance workflows, subject to the statutory applicability of each customer and activity.
- Digital MRV, activity data, measurements, evidence management, reconciliation and traceability.
- Methodology execution, reporting, assurance workflow and registry integrations, without claiming independent verifier or regulator authority.
- Organisation, project, facility and site operations with auditable access and tenant boundaries.
- Interoperable APIs, data exchange and partner integrations.

### Paid services
- DPI and public-sector integration architecture and implementation.
- Government/ULB/PSU and enterprise onboarding, configuration and deployment.
- Connector, data migration, API and interoperability engineering.
- Data quality, mapping, reconciliation and evidence-pack preparation.
- Managed platform operations, hosting, monitoring, backup, support and service-level agreements.
- Training, operational enablement and partner/OEM integration.
- Customer-specific reporting and analytics services.

The charging basis is engineering, licensed commercial software, deployment, integration, hosting, support and contracted operating services. Do not charge for favourable verification, statutory approval, regulator acceptance or public entitlement decisions.

## 6. Government-led ecosystem deployment model

The target is to make RupayKg deployable as a governed service or application layer within public programmes and government-led digital ecosystems, subject to the programme's authority, procurement, architecture, data-sharing and security requirements.

The integration posture is:

- connect through authorised APIs, documented specifications and approved exchange mechanisms;
- respect the government programme's operator, identity, consent, access, data-residency and retention rules;
- keep RupayKg's customer/tenant data and workflow boundaries explicit;
- separate public reference data, government-controlled data, customer-provided data and proprietary operational data;
- provide data portability and audit trails while respecting lawful restrictions;
- avoid direct database coupling to an external DPI or government system;
- do not infer access rights from an open API specification or a public repository listing;
- preserve independent statutory, verifier, regulator and public-sector decision authority;
- require programme sponsorship, security review, data-sharing authority, contract and acceptance before production integration.

A government-led ecosystem deployment does not automatically make RupayKg a DPI. Any formal DPI role, public operator status, delegated authority or official registry function would require a separate institutional and legal basis.

## 7. National-first architecture and Jabalpur validation

The existing national-first rule continues to govern:

**Build one national RupayKg platform. Deploy and validate it first at Jabalpur SITE-JBP-01 Kathonda. Scale using governed configuration, federated integrations and repeatable operations—not through a separate pilot architecture.**

DPGA technology candidates and GDPIR architecture lessons must be assessed against this national architecture. Jabalpur is a validation environment for selected site workflows and conditions; it is not by itself evidence of national deployment readiness, nationwide statutory applicability, multi-state acceptance, capacity or TRL 9.

See `docs/RUPAYKG_NATIONAL_DEPLOYMENT_ARCHITECTURE_AND_JABALPUR_VALIDATION.md`.

## 8. Commercial decision gates

A candidate integration or deployment may proceed only when all applicable questions have evidence:

| Gate | Required decision/evidence |
|---|---|
| Strategic fit | Which national RupayKg capability or customer need does it advance? |
| Source evidence | Is the DPGA/GDPIR source, record, date and exact artefact documented? |
| Rights | Are software, data, standards and IP rights compatible with the intended use? |
| Legal/institutional | Is the operating role, data-sharing authority, procurement route and statutory scope valid? |
| Architecture | Does it use versioned interfaces and preserve national tenancy, security and portability? |
| Security/privacy | Have data flows, threat model, access, secrets, retention and safeguards been reviewed? |
| Delivery | Are implementation owner, dependencies, support, service levels and exit plan defined? |
| Economics | Is there a buyer, budget, priced scope, implementation cost, ongoing cost and viable margin? |
| Acceptance | Are test criteria, field evidence and independent approvals defined and met? |
| Scale | Can the solution be repeated across jurisdictions without a bespoke code fork? |

Use explicit statuses: **RESEARCH, WATCH, PROTOTYPE, CUSTOMER-VALIDATION, APPROVED-FOR-IMPLEMENTATION, LIVE, RETIRED**. Do not describe a research or prototype item as adopted/live.

## 9. Commercial performance measures

Track both commercial and infrastructure-delivery performance:

- recurring revenue, ARR, renewal rate and revenue mix;
- qualified public-sector and enterprise pipeline, win rate, sales cycle and contract value;
- implementation revenue, delivery margin and time-to-deploy;
- active paying organisations, jurisdictions, facilities and sites;
- integration reuse rate and time/cost per new connector;
- availability, latency, support response, incident resolution and recovery performance;
- cost-to-serve by organisation, facility, transaction and data volume;
- data quality, evidence completeness, reconciliation exceptions and auditability;
- partner-sourced revenue and partner delivery quality;
- customer portability, data export and exit completion.

Do not invent market size, price points, adoption numbers or public-sector demand. Validate them with buyers, procurement documents, signed agreements and actual delivery costs.

## 10. Boundaries and assurance

- DPGA recognition is not DPI designation, government endorsement, statutory approval, procurement approval or security certification.
- GDPIR inclusion is not product certification, procurement endorsement or proof that a specific model is legally transferable to India.
- An open standard or DPG licence does not automatically make third-party data, hosted services or all associated components open.
- Public APIs and reference architectures do not confer access rights.
- RupayKg's internal verification workflow does not replace legally authorised independent verification or regulator decisions.
- Production deployment, national readiness, regulatory compliance, field acceptance and TRL are separately evidenced under SHAKTI Standards and existing production gates.
- Do not change production databases, tenant/RLS controls, credentials, release settings or live integrations solely as a result of ecosystem research.

## 11. Controlling strategic statement

**DPGA helps us discover reusable technology. GDPIR helps us learn how digital public infrastructure is governed and deployed. RupayKg turns that knowledge into an interoperable environmental operating platform and paid integration services. DIGICORE INFRASYS builds, owns or lawfully controls, implements and commercialises the engineering, products and managed services.**

The objective is a commercially sustainable RupayKg that can operate within government-led digital ecosystems while preserving lawful institutional boundaries, interoperable interfaces, customer trust and DIGICORE INFRASYS's defensible commercial value.

**Document status:** Internal strategic direction and operating framework. Not a legal opinion, proof of IP ownership, government partnership, DPI recognition, customer contract or production acceptance.
